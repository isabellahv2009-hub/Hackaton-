# 🌍 World Ready: Open Education in Geography, History & English

> Free, open-source teaching materials and a shared framework so that every child and teen, in every country, learns where the world is, how it got here, and how to talk to it.

---

## 1. The Problem

Students around the world finish school without a solid grasp of three basics they need to understand and participate in a connected world:

- **Geography:** where countries, oceans, and cultures are, and why places matter. Gaps here lead to embarrassment, stereotyping, and sometimes bullying, such as the online mockery that students and adults from many countries (the U.S. among them) face for geography mistakes. Shaming is not the answer; **better teaching is.**
- **History:** without context, people struggle to understand current events, other cultures, and their own communities.
- **English as a basic common language:** English is widely used for science, the internet, travel, and business. Learners without access to it are shut out of information and opportunity.

**Why it persists**
- Geography and history are often treated as "memorize and forget" subjects.
- Teachers in many regions lack free, high-quality, up-to-date materials.
- Curricula differ widely between countries, and few resources are built to be shared across borders.
- Language barriers keep good teaching resources from spreading.

## 2. The Solution

**World Ready** is an open, community-built library and framework that provides:

1. **Free lesson plans and activities** for ages 6–18 in geography, history, and basic English.
2. **A shared skills framework** (what a student should be able to do at each age) that any school, teacher, or country can adapt.
3. **Translation and localization** so materials work in many languages and respect local context.
4. **Low-tech friendly design:** printable, usable offline, and light on bandwidth.
5. **Positive learning culture:** curiosity, not shame. Mistakes are part of learning.

## 3. The Framework

### 3.1 Principles
| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Open** | All content under open licenses (e.g., CC BY-SA for content, MIT for code). |
| **Adaptable** | Every lesson can be localized to a country, language, or classroom. |
| **Skills over memorization** | Map-reading, reasoning with sources, and communication come first. |
| **Inclusive & balanced** | History from multiple perspectives; no stereotypes. |
| **Accessible** | Works offline, on paper, and on low-end devices. |

### 3.2 Three Pillars

**🗺️ Geography**
| Level | Ages | Core skills |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | 6–8 | My home, neighborhood, continents & oceans, basic map symbols |
| Explorers | 9–11 | Countries & capitals of my region, compass & scale, climate zones |
| Navigators | 12–14 | World regions, population & migration, resources, reading data/maps |
| Global Citizens | 15–18 | Geopolitics, sustainability, GIS basics, evaluating map sources |

**📜 History**
| Level | Ages | Core skills |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | 6–8 | Timelines, family & community stories |
| Explorers | 9–11 | Ancient civilizations, key local & world events |
| Navigators | 12–14 | Cause & effect, primary vs. secondary sources |
| Global Citizens | 15–18 | Multiple perspectives, historiography, connecting past to present |

**💬 English (basic common language)**
| Level | Ages | Core skills |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | 6–8 | Alphabet, greetings, numbers, classroom words |
| Explorers | 9–11 | Simple sentences, everyday vocabulary, short reading |
| Navigators | 12–14 | Conversation, paragraphs, listening, travel & internet English |
| Global Citizens | 15–18 | Clear writing, academic & professional basics |

### 3.3 Lesson Template
Every lesson in the repo follows the same structure:

```

Time needed:
Materials (low-tech version + digital version):
Warm-up (5 min):

Check for understanding:
Extension / homework:
Localization notes (how to adapt):

```

### 3.4 Quality Checklist for Contributions
- [ ] Facts verified with at least one reliable source
- [ ] Tested or reviewed by a teacher (if possible)
- [ ] No stereotypes; respectful of all cultures
- [ ] Works without internet (or has an offline alternative)
- [ ] Uses the lesson template and states its license

## 5. How to Contribute

You don't need to be a teacher or a developer. You can:

- ✍️ **Write or improve a lesson** using the template
- 🌐 **Translate** existing materials
- 🔍 **Fact-check** content and suggest sources
- 🎨 **Design** maps, worksheets, and games
- 🧑‍🏫 **Pilot** a lesson in a classroom and share feedback

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) to get started, and please follow our [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
